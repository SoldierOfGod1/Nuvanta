# Design: Nuvanta MVP — Office Hours Diagnostic

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-05-03
Mode: Startup
Status: DRAFT — pending founder customer-discovery output

## Problem Statement

Founder wants to launch Nuvanta, a SA lifestyle eCommerce brand, in 2 weeks.
Three product niches: loadshedding/power, home convenience, comfort/wellness.
Shopify dropship using local SA suppliers (Dropstore, uAfrica, Planet54).
Goal: first R10k, then scale a winning product.

The plan as written is energetic but unfocused. It has the brand, the niches, the
suppliers, the product list, and the launch sequence. It does not have a customer.

## Demand Evidence (current state)

None named in the plan. No interview transcripts, no waitlist, no pre-orders, no
specific person who has said "I would pay for this." The plan asserts demand based
on category-level trends ("urban South Africans value convenience"), which is filter
logic, not buying signal.

This is the single largest gap in the plan.

## Status Quo (the real competitor)

For loadshedding products: Takealot (next-day delivery in Joburg/Cape Town),
Makro, Builders Warehouse, Game.
For wellness: Dis-Chem, Clicks.
For home convenience: Mr Price Home, @home, Takealot Marketplace.

All have: 1-2 day delivery, established trust, easy returns, in-app purchase.
Nuvanta proposes: 2-5 day delivery, zero brand trust, paid-ad-driven discovery,
WhatsApp support.

The "Fast Delivery 🇿🇦" badge in the plan is structurally slower than the
incumbents the target customer already uses.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Plan says: "Urban South Africans 20-45, mobile-first, value convenience."
This is a census filter, not a target. Cannot be marketed to. Cannot be
interviewed. Cannot validate ad creative against.

Recommended narrowest wedge:
- ONE product: Power Bank 20,000mAh (year-round demand, low return rate,
  low support load, broad appeal, ~50% gross margin).
- ONE channel: Meta + TikTok paid social.
- ONE goal: 30 units sold OR clear "no" within 7 days, ~R3k ad budget.

## Constraints

- Bootstrapped (no funding stated).
- Solo founder (no team mentioned).
- 2-week timeline (Week 1 build, Week 2 launch).
- Local SA market only.
- Dropship-only (no inventory).

## Premises (must agree before proceeding)

1. **Loadshedding fear-of-return motivates 2026 buyers** — disagree.
   Stage 4+ events are rare and short. Demand has structurally weakened.
2. **50-65% gross margin survives unit economics** — disagree.
   Net margin after CPA, fees, returns, chargebacks is realistically 20-28%.
3. **2-5 day delivery is competitive in SA** — disagree.
   Takealot is next-day. Customers already have a faster option.
4. **Three niches reduces risk** — disagree.
   Three niches = 3x creative pipelines, 3x testing budget, 3x cognitive load.
5. **R10k revenue is the right milestone** — partially disagree.
   R10k profit is the right milestone. Specify which one.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Wedge First, Single Product, 7-Day Ship (RECOMMENDED)
- One product (Power Bank 20,000mAh), one-product Shopify store, no nav.
- R3,000 ad budget across Meta + TikTok, 2 creatives, 3 audiences.
- Goal: 30 units OR clear "no" in 7 days.
- Effort: S. Risk: Low.
- Pros: Fastest demand signal, cheapest "no", honors the plan's own "ONE
  winning product" rule.
- Cons: No brand vibe yet. Boring single-product page.

### Approach B: Takealot Marketplace Pilot
- List 3 products on Takealot Marketplace using their fulfillment + traffic.
- Compressed margin (Takealot fee 12-15%) but CPA approaches zero.
- Validate product demand without building the brand house.
- Effort: S. Risk: Lowest.
- Pros: Real customers, real reviews, no ad spend, no Shopify.
- Cons: No customer email captured, no brand equity built.

### Approach C: Content-Led, Audience First
- 30 days TikTok + IG content as "Loadshedding Survival SA" before any store.
- Goal: 5,000 followers + 500 email subs.
- Then survey the list and sell what they ask for.
- Effort: M. Risk: Med.
- Pros: Defensible audience asset that competitors can't copy.
- Cons: 30 days before first revenue. Conflicts with "speed" mandate.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A — Wedge First.**

Rationale: the plan already states "focus on ONE winning product" but contradicts
itself by launching with 9 SKUs across 3 niches. Approach A makes the plan
internally consistent and produces a real demand signal in 7 days for ~R3k of risk.
Brand expansion to lifestyle / 3 niches becomes Phase 2, only after a winning SKU
exists.

## Success Criteria

Phase 0 (this design doc): founder completes 5 customer interviews per the
Assignment below.

Phase 1 (after interviews): single-product wedge launched. 30 units sold OR
documented "no" within 7 days of launch.

Phase 2 (only if Phase 1 succeeds): expand to 3 SKUs in the winning niche.

Phase 3 (only if Phase 2 succeeds): consider second niche.

## Distribution Plan

Shopify Basic (R580/mo) for the storefront.
PayFast + Ozow for payments.
Dropstore for fulfillment.
Meta Ads + TikTok Ads for traffic.
WhatsApp Business for support.
Code-light: no custom backend until Phase 2.

## The Assignment

Before writing one line of Shopify config, do this in the next 48 hours:

Talk to 5 specific people who currently own a Mini UPS or 20,000mAh power bank.
Ask:
1. Where did you buy it? (Takealot? Makro? Amazon? Dropship?)
2. What made you finally buy it? (Trigger event)
3. Would you buy a second one as a gift? At what price?
4. If a new SA brand sold the same thing for R100 less but took 5 days to
   arrive, would you wait?

Bring back five names and their answers. Then we plan the store.

## What I noticed about how you think

- "Speed > Perfection" sits at the bottom of the doc. The top has 9 SKUs across
  3 niches with a full nav, testimonials, and benefits bar. The plan's instinct
  is right. The plan's execution is too wide.
- "This is not a side hustle. This is a real business." This line is doing
  emotional work. Real businesses have a customer name first.
- The brand name (Nuvanta) is the first thing in the doc, before the customer.
  Founders who pick the name first usually fall in love with the brand and
  resist killing it. Be ready to kill Nuvanta if the customer says no.
- "Honest delivery timelines" appears under risk mitigation. The honest
  timeline (2-5 days) is *worse than Takealot's*. The plan's own honesty is
  the customer's reason to leave.
