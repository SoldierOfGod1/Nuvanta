# Founder Time + Cash Budget — Nuvanta

Generated 2026-05-04. The honest 90-day plan for time and money.

Most SA dropship stores don't fail from bad strategy. They fail because
the founder runs out of time, energy, or cash before the strategy gets
to play out. This doc is the cold-water reality check on all three.

### What cash you must have available

**Minimum viable starting cash: R30,000.** This funds setup + 2 wedge
attempts + cushion if Phase 2 takes 6 weeks not 4.

**Comfortable starting cash: R50,000.** Adds a third wedge attempt,
attorney IP filing, and 30-day ad-spend reserve when scale ramps.

**If you have less than R20,000 right now:** do not launch yet.
Build cash to R30k first (60-90 days of saving / freelancing).
Launching under-capitalised is the most common failure mode in SA
dropship.

## 3. Energy budget (the silent killer)

Cash and hours are measurable. Energy isn't, and that's why founders
miss it.

### Watch for these energy leaks
1. **Customer support emotional load** — angry customers compound.
   Hire VA at 30 tickets/day, not 50.
2. **Decision fatigue** — every product pick, ad creative, copy choice,
   reply tone is a decision. By Friday afternoon decisions get worse.
   Front-load big decisions to Monday morning.
3. **Creative fatigue** — making 3 ads/week sustainably is harder than
   making 6 in a launch week. Plan a creative-source cycle: every 4
   weeks, do a 2-day shoot to bank 8-10 raw clips you can edit on
   demand.
4. **Cash anxiety** — checking the bank balance daily kills creativity.
   Check Mondays only.
5. **Comparison fatigue** — checking competitor stores, Twitter
   founder-flexes, friends' "how's the business going?" questions.
   Mute. Block. Disengage. Compare your week 6 to your week 2, not to
   someone's week 60.

### Energy preservation rules
- One real day off per week (no email, no Shopify, no Meta Ads Manager)
- Sleep before midnight 6 nights of 7
- Move physically once a day (walk, run, gym — anything)
- One non-Nuvanta human conversation per day that isn't customer support

If any three of these slip for 2 weeks straight, you're in burnout
territory. See `/docs/15-risk-register.md` B3.

## 4. The 90-day decision gate

At day 90, run this honest accounting. Sit with it for an evening.

### Quantitative
- [ ] Net contribution covers monthly fixed costs (R1,270)?
- [ ] Net contribution > R5,000/mo (= founder is paying themselves
      something, even if minimum-wage equivalent)?
- [ ] Repeat purchase rate > 8% (sign that brand has any pull)?
- [ ] Email list > 800 subscribers (audience asset taking shape)?
- [ ] CPA stable < R200 across ad spend (sustainable economics)?

### Qualitative
- [ ] Founder still excited to ship product picks?
- [ ] Customer feedback (the day-7 email replies) positive?
- [ ] Founder slept, exercised, and saw friends in the last 30 days?
- [ ] Brand voice still feels authentic to read aloud?
- [ ] Founder believes the next 90 days will be better than the last?

### The gate
- **5+ quantitative + 4+ qualitative checked:** keep going. You're
  building a real brand.
- **3-4 quant + 3-4 qual checked:** review cost structure, hire
  support, simplify SKU lineup. Continue with adjusted plan.
- **0-2 quant + 0-2 qual checked:** declare it a learning project.
  Wind down with dignity. Refund anything pending. Public note that
  thanks customers. The lessons compound to the next venture.

There is no shame in column 3. Most founders who ship a 90-day learning
project are stronger founders 12 months later than ones who quietly
limped along for 18 months.

## 5. The simplest possible math

If after 90 days the brand is doing R100k/mo revenue with 25% net
contribution, that's R25k/mo to the founder. Below market wage in SA
for a senior employee, but a real business with optionality.

If after 90 days the brand is doing R20k/mo revenue with 20% net
contribution, that's R4k/mo. A part-time hobby cash flow. Fine if you
treat it as one. Not fine if you're treating it as your full-time job.

Be honest about which you're building. Both are valid.

## 6. Founder pay (when you can start)

In Phase 1: **founder pays themselves R0.** Every rand of contribution
goes back into ad spend.

In Phase 2 (week 8 onwards): if monthly net contribution > R10k,
founder pays themselves R3k-R5k/mo. Symbolic but important —
disciplines the brand to operate as a business not a money pit.

In Phase 3 (month 4+): founder pay scales with sustainable monthly
contribution. Rule of thumb: founder pay = 30% of monthly net
contribution, capped at R25k/mo until brand is on infrastructure that
runs without daily founder presence.

In Phase 4 (month 7+): if hitting R200k+/mo revenue with healthy
margin, founder pay can rise to market-rate. Reinvest the rest.

## 7. The unsexy truth

Most SA dropship stores fail because the founder ran out of cash at
day 60-90, right before any wedge would have worked.

Have R30k. Run the plan. If it doesn't work, walk away with the lessons.
If it works, you've got a brand worth building for years.

Don't run this on R5k of "let's see what happens." That's not a
business plan. That's a lottery ticket.
